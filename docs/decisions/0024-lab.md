# 0024. The Lab: a benchmark, a private diary and a label reader

Status: accepted, M1.

## Context

Hearthwake is also an experiment in what the web can do with AI on the device. The same engine, one LLM loaded in the page plus MediaPipe vision and system speech, serves more than waking things. Three small experiments show it and help measure devices.

## Decision

A Lab (`#/lab`), linked from the hearth, holds three experiments.

- **How fast is this device?** (`#/bench`)
  - Loads the chosen model and has it write a short story (at most 128 tokens).
  - Reads WebLLM's own speed report (`runtimeStatsText`): prefill and decode tokens per second.
  - Also measures time to first token and model load, and times the classifier and the embedder over five runs.
  - The result can be shared or copied with a one-line summary.
  - There is no server, so results are compared by sharing them; the ones we collect go to `docs/models.md`.
- **Private diary** (`#/diary`)
  - Entries are typed or dictated with system speech recognition, and kept in IndexedDB (database version 2 adds a `diary` store).
  - "Reflect on my week" gives the model the last twelve entries, with how many days ago each was written. It returns a short reflection, one thing that went well and one gentle question. It never judges or gives medical advice.
- **Label reader** (`#/reader`)
  - Photographs a label, menu or sign and reads the text on the device with Tesseract (`tesseract.js`, English and Spanish).
  - Tesseract's worker, core and language data come from its CDN on first use; the worker is shut down after each read to free its memory.
  - The text can be corrected, then asked about with quick questions (what is it, allergens, a translation, is it vegan) or a free one.
  - The prompt warns the model that OCR makes mistakes, and asks it to send people to the package itself for allergies.

## Consequences

- The label reader downloads Tesseract's files (about 10 MB) on first use. It holds them in memory beside the LLM only while reading.
- Dictation in the diary passes through the system speech recognizer, which may use its maker's servers (ADR 0017). Typed entries and the model's reading stay on the device.
- The mock engine answers each experiment with canned text, so the e2e tests cover all three.
